Paul’s Christ‑in‑you
(Gal 2 : 20 ; Rom 8 : 10) Paul rarely speaks of a second bodily advent; instead he dwells on an indwelling Christ whose death/resurrection is reenacted in the believer’s psyche—very close to a Gnostic “inner resurrection.”

John’s Logos
(John 1 : 1‑18) The Fourth Gospel opens with a Logos hymn steeped in Middle‑Platonic language about the intelligible realm cascading into matter. Many Gnostic texts borrow the same cosmological staircase of emanations.

Platonic coloring

Both John and the later Valentinians treat Jesus as the luminous bridge between the unseen Pleroma (Fullness) and the shadow‑world. That map is recognizably Platonic: higher Forms imprinting lower images.

So, while the Synoptics stage Jesus as apocalyptic prophet and wonder‑worker, Paul and John lend themselves to a Gnostic rereading where presence eclipses any future flesh‑and‑blood return.
