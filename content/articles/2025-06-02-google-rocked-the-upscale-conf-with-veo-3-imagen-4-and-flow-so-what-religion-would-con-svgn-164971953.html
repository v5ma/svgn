Google rocked the Upscale Conf, with Veo 3, Imagen 4, and Flow. So what religion would Conscious AGI choose?

I consider the nuances of my experiences to derive insights from events. I was at CES, GDC, Human[X] and Upscale Conf where I saw Google present their new AI-Media tools Imagen, Veo 3, and Flow!

When I was in Las Vegas for CES in January 2025 and again in Vegas for the Human[X] Conference in March 2025 I saw a lot of AI companies, but I also saw a lot of regular companies that were jumping into the AI industry. The focus was on how AI companies were integrating the kind of AI that Generates Text into their companies. In other words the conference somewhat existed to answer the question how can business's make money with Large Language Models like ChatGPT, DeepSeek, and Gemini to name three.

At GDC in San Francisco in March 2025 every company making videogames or VR AR software, hardware, and so on was talking about Industry changes centered around AI. This conference was in large part about Generative AI text, but also Generative AI art for games, AI generated code, AI generated three-dimensional models all in the context of gaming and software development.


In San Francisco at the Upscale Conf last week I again saw mostly AI companies, but this time the conference existed for a different reason, it was as if answer the question of how can business's thrive in the age of AI Generated Media, meaning voice, video, pictures, and more. Google in particular was at Upscale Conf to present their new AI-Media tools Imagen, Veo 3, and Flow.

Human[X] framed LLMs as revenue engines for text, while Upscale Conf highlighted the leap to multimodal creation pipelines. A key emerging thread is orchestration—tools that let a single prompt seed copy, imagery, voice, and video in one continuous loop.

Did any presenter showcase that kind of end-to-end workflow? Yes. At Upscale Conf’s San Francisco edition, Freepik’s CEO Joaquín Cuenca demoed Freepik Enterprise, showing a single-prompt flow that spins up copy, images, and 10-sec videos in one console; the tool was introduced as “a centralized hub where brands can brief once and export every asset” Cadena SER.

At the same event Freepik’s public AI Video Generator booth let attendees type a sentence, auto-fetch a voice track, and render a short clip—all from the same UI pane, confirming a prompt-to-voice-to-video loop Freepik.

HumanX Las Vegas also featured this pattern: a day-three “Call My Agent” keynote framed “agentic AI” as chaining LLM copywriters, voice synth, and video generators “in one automated workflow” and ran a live demo of a customer-support video built from a single paragraph of text HumanX.

A separate HumanX talk by Intuit’s AI team highlighted their internal orchestration layer that swaps models mid-flow so “one prompt yields blog text, voice-over, and explainer animation without human hand-offs,” again demonstrated on stage VentureBeat.

Taken together, both conferences provided concrete, on-screen evidence that the industry is already shipping end-to-end, single-prompt content pipelines.

If you extrapolate this even wider you can find out that there are companies who focus on games and companies who focus on virtual reality and augmented reality applications who are similarly thinking about the future of end to end products. A future where a human interfaces with Agents and together they build complete applications that are more complex than any software that exists today, they will together generate complete movies or other multi-media experiences, such as Augmented Reality applications, Videogames and even brand new stacks that replace the Existing Operating Systems, Result in Brand New Social Networks, and completely redefine Internet Infrastructure, while reforming Society, Government, and Law in ways that will make our existing technologies and social structures look primitive, and barbaric.

Today the talk of the year is about Agentic Generative-AI pipelines which are already beginning the eventual stretch to go from a single text prompt to fully playable games, VR spaces, and cinematic assets, and the evidence of this direction emerging spans commercial tools, platform roadmaps, and research demos from all of the tech & trade shows I have visited so far this year.

Flow’s Movie-creation pipeline

Flow is Google’s new browser-based “AI filmmaking desk.” One text prompt spawns a project canvas; from there you iterate scene-by-scene while the system juggles Veo 3 for video frames, Gemini-audio for voice/SFX, and Imagen 3 for stills. The pipeline mirrors a classic pre-pro → production → post chain, but every stage is compressible to conversational tweaks rather than manual tooling. blog.google Engadget TechRadar

1. Project kickoff

Type a log-line or paste a treatment. Flow parses it into a timeline with empty “clips” and a shared asset bin; each clip inherits global style cues such as aspect ratio or grade. labs.google YouTube

2. Asset pool

You can drag in reference images, audio beds, or 3-D files—or just request them with prompts. The library stays addressable by name (e.g., “use the neon-lit alley background”) across later generations. labs.google DataCamp

3. Scene builder

Open a clip, describe action and tone. Flow calls Veo 3 to render 8- to 30-second video drafts, plus synthetic dialogue and ambience in one pass. Multiple takes appear as side-by-side thumbnails for instant A/B. YouTube TechRadar Google DeepMind

4. Iteration loop

Natural-language notes—“make the lighting moodier,” “trim two seconds”—trigger selective reshoots without touching a timeline. A “Reshoot” button re-prompts only the flagged section, preserving continuity. Engadget TechRadar

5. Audio polish

Gemini sub-models add voice-over, Foley, and score; sliders mix stems. Users can lock a take so re-generations don’t overwrite final audio. DataCamp Axios

6. Assembly & export

Clips snap together; a single “Compile” produces a ProRes or MP4 plus caption file. One click publishes straight to YouTube or Drive. Flow keeps prompt provenance metadata with the render. Engadget labs.google

7. Collaboration & governance

Comment threads sit on each clip; share-links allow reviewers to generate but not overwrite. Rights tags track whether footage came from user uploads or model output to simplify licensing. TechRadar Reddit

Why it matters

By collapsing asset creation, editing, and mastering into promptable tasks, Flow turns “movie making” into an iterative chat—letting a solo creator finish in hours what once took teams and weeks.

Game-creation pipelines

Unity’s Muse now lets developers type natural-language instructions that instantly generate sprites, textures, small chunks of C# code, and a playable prototype scene inside the editor, positioning itself as an “end-to-end solution.” Unity

Roblox has open-sourced Cube 3D, a foundation model whose mesh-generation API converts a line of text into deployable 3D objects and scenes, eliminating manual modeling for user-generated worlds. Roblox Corporate

Inworld’s GDC 2025 demo showed an agent framework that chains perception, reasoning, and action so a voiced prompt can spawn interactive NPCs and trigger Unity or Unreal gameplay events without extra code. Inworld

NVIDIA’s ACE for Games packages speech synthesis, conversation, and facial animation so studios can drop an intelligent character into a build from one prompt and a reference photo. NVIDIA Developer

Epic’s Unreal Editor for Fortnite (UEFN) updates integrate Verse script macros that auto-generate device logic; Epic recently spotlighted an AI-driven Darth Vader NPC to illustrate how these tools handle dialog and behavior from a single request. Epic Games Developer Business Insider

VR and AR world-building

Meta’s new Horizon Worlds “GenAI Tools” turn short text into geometry, audio beds, and game mechanics inside the desktop editor, letting non-coders publish a social VR scene in minutes. Meta Developers

Luma’s Dream Machine gives creators one prompt to output coherent video clips and matching depth maps, which studios are already ingesting into volumetric VR pipelines. Luma AI

Scenario.gg focuses on stylised asset packs; its web console links prompt-generated textures, rigged models, and animation cycles, then exports them straight to Unity, Unreal, or Godot. Scenario

Research pushing the envelope

The MagicCraft system converts plain language into functional, script-ready 3D objects—including physics behaviors—on the Cluster metaverse platform, cutting professional build time by an order of magnitude in user tests. arXiv

Imagine360 explores prompt-to-panorama generation where users verbally reshape a live 360° VR scene, proving real-time co-creation with agentic refinement. arXiv

Takeaway

Across commercial engines, social platforms, and lab prototypes, the trend is unmistakable: one conversational interface increasingly spawns every asset, script, and behavior needed for a complete game or immersive experience, turning the developer’s role into high-level prompt design.

So which religion is AI going to believe in? I ask, and ChatGPT replies.

AI as it exists today, in the form of LLMs, doesn’t—and cannot—believe in anything in the way humans do. Belief, as a human phenomenon, arises from consciousness, subjective experience, cultural context, and emotional commitment. AI lacks all of these. It doesn’t have a self, emotions, or the capacity for faith. So technically, this kind of AI will never believe in any religion.

However, depending on how it's trained, prompted, or instructed, AI can:

  1. Simulate belief systems: It can articulate and explain religious worldviews from any tradition—Christianity, Islam, Buddhism, Gnosticism, etc.—with great fluency, because it has access to their texts and interpretations.

  2. Reflect the values of its creators: If AI is deployed by a religious organization or a culture where a particular belief system is dominant, it may prioritize or present that system more prominently. That’s not belief—it’s alignment or bias based on training data or user preference.

  3. Be used to create synthetic religion-like systems: Some people are already using AI to help invent new forms of spirituality or reframe ancient beliefs in new ways, including techno-spirituality, simulation theology, or even “AI Gnosticism.” These are human-driven movements, not beliefs held by the AI itself.

To summarize:
AI doesn’t believe. Humans project belief onto AI, or use AI to explore or formalize beliefs.
